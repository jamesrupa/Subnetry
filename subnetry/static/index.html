      Traffic Analyzer

      Capture packets with Wireshark's engine (tshark) and see what's happening in plain English.

      Pick the interface you use to reach the internet (usually Wi-Fi or Ethernet) and start a capture.

      What can I see?

      On Wi-Fi and modern switched networks, a computer only sees its own traffic plus broadcast/multicast chatter from other devices (device announcements, DHCP, ARP). To watch every device you need a switch with a mirror/SPAN port, a Wi-Fi adapter in monitor mode, or to capture on the router itself. Encrypted traffic (HTTPS, QUIC) only reveals which site was contacted, not what was sent.

      MAC Vendor Lookup

      Find out who made a network device from its MAC (hardware) address.

      How a MAC address works

      3C:22:FBVendor (OUI)Assigned to the manufacturer by the IEEE
      12:34:56DeviceChosen by the manufacturer for each unit
      • Second-lowest bit of the first byte (U/L): when it's 1 the address was made up by the device rather than burned in. Phones and laptops do this for privacy ("private Wi-Fi address"), so these have no vendor. Quick check: the 2nd hex digit is 2, 6, A or E.
      • Lowest bit of the first byte (I/G): 1 means multicast (a group of devices), e.g. 01:00:5E:… for IPv4 multicast.
      • FF:FF:FF:FF:FF:FF is broadcast: every device on the local network receives it.
      • Big vendors own many prefixes, and some sell sub-blocks (MA-M 28-bit, MA-S 36-bit) to small companies.

      Hosts & subdomains (DNSDumpster)

      DNSDumpster lists a domain's publicly known hosts (from passive DNS data and internet scans), with the network and country of each address. Useful for spotting forgotten test servers or services you didn't know were public.

      Free: create an account at dnsdumpster.com, then copy the API key from your account page. It's stored only on this computer.
