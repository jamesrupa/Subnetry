Health Check

A score, and the three changes that raise it

The full scan runs a speed test, a traceroute, a device and port scan and a Wi-Fi scan in a few minutes. Each problem costs points, and the fixes are ranked by how much they're worth.

  • Spots open services like Telnet, Remote Desktop and unprotected databases
  • Saves an HTML report plus CSV lists of your devices and Wi-Fi networks

Wi-Fi

The quietest channel, on every band

See every network around you on 2.4, 5 and 6 GHz, with signal in dBm and which channels overlap. Subnetry recommends 1, 6 or 11 on 2.4 GHz, a channel without radar checks on 5 GHz, and a preferred scanning channel on 6 GHz.

  • Ignores your own mesh nodes when counting neighbours
  • Wi-Fi Monitor catches weak spots and devices clinging to a distant access point

Traceroute

See the road your traffic takes

Every hop to a website, with its name, the network that runs it and its country. Subnetry tells you whether delay starts at your router, at your internet provider, or is simply the distance to another continent.

  • Explains which silent hops are normal and which packet loss is real
  • Works the same on Mac and Windows

Toolkit

The reference tools you keep opening tabs for

A subnet calculator that shows network and host bits in binary, a DNS lookup that decodes SPF, DKIM and DMARC, a MAC vendor lookup and a reference of common ports and how risky they are to leave open.

  • Works offline, except DNS lookups
  • Handy for learning networking as well as fixing it

Install

Up and running in a minute

  1. Open the .dmg and drag Subnetry onto the Applications folder.
  2. Open Subnetry from Applications or Launchpad.
  3. If macOS says it can't verify Subnetry, click Done, then open System Settings › Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. On macOS 14 and earlier you can instead right-click the app and choose Open. You only do this once.
  4. Allow it to find devices on your local network when macOS asks. The scanners need it.
  5. Optional: open the Wi-Fi Scanner and press Allow location access. macOS only shows Wi-Fi network names to apps with Location access; Subnetry never uses your location.
  1. Run the installer. It installs for your user account, so it doesn't need administrator rights.
  2. If SmartScreen says "Windows protected your PC", click More info › Run anyway.
  3. Tick the optional tools you want (Speedtest.net CLI, Nmap, Wireshark). Their own installers open; keep Npcap ticked.
  4. Open Subnetry from the Start menu or the desktop shortcut.
  5. For Wi-Fi names, turn on Settings › Privacy & security › Location, including "Let desktop apps access your location".

The security prompts appear because Subnetry isn't code-signed by Apple or Microsoft yet. To update, download the new version and install it over the old one; your saved reports are kept.

Privacy

It runs on your computer, not in the cloud

There's no account and no tracking. Scan results and reports are saved only on your computer, in a folder called Subnetry-Reports.

Some tests have to reach the internet. These are the only times Subnetry does:

  • Speed testsSpeedtest.net or Cloudflare test servers
  • Your public IPipinfo.io, ipwho.is and ipify, to show your provider and location
  • DNS lookupsThe DNS server you choose (your network's, Cloudflare, Google or Quad9)
  • Traceroute namesTeam Cymru's free service, to name the network behind each hop
  • Only if you use themDNSDumpster with your own key, and the IEEE device-maker list when you press Update

Only scan networks and devices you own or have permission to test. Port scans of other people's systems can break their terms of use or the law.

Questions

FAQ

Is Subnetry free?

Yes. There are no paid tiers, ads or accounts.

Why does my Mac or PC warn me when I first open it?

Apple and Microsoft show a warning for apps that aren't signed with a paid developer certificate. Subnetry isn't signed yet, so you confirm once that you want to open it (see Install above). After that it opens normally.

Why can't I see Wi-Fi network names?

macOS and Windows 11 hide nearby network names from apps without Location permission, because they could reveal where you are. Allow location access when asked. Subnetry uses it only to read Wi-Fi details.

Can it see what other people on my network are doing?

No. On Wi-Fi and modern home networks a computer only sees its own traffic plus announcements other devices broadcast. The Network Scanner finds devices and their open ports; it doesn't read anyone's traffic.

Does it change my router settings?

Never. Subnetry only measures and recommends. You make any change yourself, in your router's settings.

Does it work on Linux?

Yes, from source: it needs Python 3.10 or newer. The instructions are on GitHub.

How do I uninstall it?

Mac: drag Subnetry from Applications to the Trash. Windows: Settings › Apps › Installed apps › Subnetry › Uninstall. Your reports stay in the Subnetry-Reports folder until you delete it.
